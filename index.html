Featured project

Where should a food delivery platform expand next?

I analysed 148,541 Swiggy restaurant listings to find cities and cuisines where customer demand is high but few restaurants serve it, and turned the result into expansion recommendations for a business audience.

Tool
R, in a Quarto document
Data
Swiggy Restaurants Dataset from Kaggle, 148,541 rows
Code
github.com/Shubhi-1001/swiggy-restaurant-analysis

Cleaning the data came first

The raw file could not be analysed as it was. Four problems needed a decision each:

  • Missing ratings. About 58% of restaurants had “--” instead of a rating. These matched the restaurants labelled “Too Few Ratings”, so the gap is structural, not random. I kept those rows and handled the gap explicitly instead of dropping them.
  • Rating counts as labels. Counts such as “20+ ratings” and “1K+ ratings” were converted to numeric estimates so I could measure demand.
  • Cost outliers. Cost was stored as text with a currency symbol, and one listing exceeded ₹300,000 for two people. Values above the 99th percentile were set to missing, and the rest of each row was kept.
  • Mixed city fields. Entries such as “Vastrapur,Ahmedabad” combined locality and city. I split them so results could be grouped by city.

What I found

Price does not predict quality

The correlation between cost and rating is 0.136, which is weak. Charging more does not reliably earn a higher rating, so a premium positioning is not a safe basis for an expansion strategy.

Ratings are steady across cities, prices are not

Average ratings sit between 3.79 and 4.08 in every major city, while average cost ranges from ₹247 to ₹358. City differences on the platform come from pricing, not from how well restaurants are rated.

Two cuisines dominate the platform

Chinese (36,464 listings) and North Indian (32,537) are far ahead of every other cuisine. On Indian delivery apps, “Chinese” usually means Indo-Chinese food such as Gobi Manchurian and Hakka noodles, and I read it that way.

The best openings are where demand outruns supply

I ranked city and cuisine pairs by an opportunity score, using only pairs with at least 20 restaurants. Vizag has two separate openings: Tandoor (score 55.5) and American (50.5). Thiruvananthapuram has the strongest single signal in the data, Arabian cuisine (54.4), and appears three times in the top 10, across Arabian, Indian and North Indian. Mumbai and Hyderabad both show under-served demand for Home Food.

What I would recommend

  1. Prioritise Vizag for near-term metro expansion. Two independent signals suggest the city is under-served broadly, not in one niche.
  2. Treat Thiruvananthapuram as a strategic emerging-market bet. Appearing under three cuisines points to headroom across the whole city.
  3. Do not rely on premium pricing to stand out. With a cost and rating correlation of 0.136, it is unlikely to earn better ratings.
  4. Monitor single-city, single-cuisine signals. Indore and Surat for Mughlai are worth watching, but they are isolated findings and not a basis for a first wave.

How I checked my own work

My first version required only 5 restaurants per city and cuisine. That let one popular restaurant lift the average for a whole cuisine, which gave misleading results. I raised the minimum to 20 and analysed established metros (1,000 or more restaurants) separately from emerging markets, since they are different decisions with different risks.

One limit remains: about 58% of restaurants have no rating yet, so rating-based findings describe rated restaurants only.
